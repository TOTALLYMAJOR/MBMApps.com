Every MBMApps product exists because of a pain point operators describe in almost the same words. We went looking for the credible, citable evidence behind those descriptions. Every number below survived source verification; the figures that failed are listed at the end so they never enter anyone's pitch deck.

## Quoting speed decides deals

Businesses that contact a new web inquiry **within one hour are roughly seven times likelier to qualify it** than those that wait even an hour longer — and more than sixty times likelier than those that wait a day. Yet in an audit of 2,241 U.S. companies, only 37% responded within an hour, 23% never responded at all, and the average first response took **42 hours** ([Harvard Business Review, 1.25M-lead study](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads)).

On the proposal side, vendor-reported platform data from roughly 90,500 signed contracts shows proposals delivered **within 48 hours closing at 88%**, against 69.6% for firms taking sixty days or more ([D-Tools 2025 Year in Review](https://www.d-tools.com/resource-center/faster-proposal-turnaround-time-results-in-higher-close-rate)). The sample is systems integrators, not caterers — the correlation travels by analogy, and simpler deals do close faster — but the direction is consistent with everything else in this register: the quote that goes out today is the one that closes.

This is the thesis behind QuoteFlow's guided quoting and QuietPilot's inquiry-to-proposal queue.

## Catering runs understaffed

Nearly **89% of event-industry leaders** say staffing shortages directly impacted their ability to deliver successful events in the past year, and only **14% of caterers** reported being well-staffed heading into 2024 ([Catersource, State of the Catering & Events Industry](https://www.catersource.com/business-operations/the-state-of-the-catering-events-industries-in-2025)). You cannot fix the labor market — but you can see coverage gaps before the event does, which is exactly what QuietPilot's staffing and readiness review keeps visible.

## Youth sports runs on overloaded parents and shrinking volunteer pools

The Aspen Institute's Project Play surveys — primary, non-commercial research — put numbers on what every league administrator already knows:

- Sports parents spend an average of **3 hours 23 minutes on every practice or game day** ([Project Play parent survey](https://projectplay.org/news/project-play-survey-parents-spend-3-hours-on-sports-every-day-their-child-plays)).
- Families spent an average of **$1,016 on a child's primary sport in 2024, up 46% since 2019**, with total parent spending on youth sports estimated above $40B annually ([Project Play, 2025](https://projectplay.org/news/2025/2/24/project-play-survey-family-spending-on-youth-sports-rises-46-over-five-years)).
- Of roughly 6.5 million youth coaches — mostly volunteers — **fewer than 1 in 5 are trained** in effective motivational technique, and formal volunteering in America fell to 23.2% between 2019 and 2021, the largest drop the Census Bureau has ever recorded ([State of Play, coaching trends](https://projectplay.org/state-of-play-2025/coaching-trends)).

Parents already give three hours a day and a thousand dollars a year; leagues run on volunteers they cannot afford to burn out. LeaguePilot carries the coordination — schedules, RSVPs, communication — so coaches can just coach.

## The kill list

Three plausible-sounding figures failed adversarial verification and should not be cited anywhere: the "88% of proposals close within 14 days" framing (the 88% attaches to 48-hour delivery), the claim that "only one in four integrators deliver proposals within two weeks," and a "515% traffic lift from content refreshing" statistic. If you have seen these numbers elsewhere, treat them as unverified.

## How to read this register

Vendor-published data (D-Tools, Jobber) is correlational and labeled as such; the HBR and Project Play findings are primary research. We hold our own marketing to the same standard we build into the products: accepted is not paid, correlation is not causation, and a claim without a source does not ship.
